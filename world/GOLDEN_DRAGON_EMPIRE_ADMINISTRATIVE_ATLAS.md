# Merzkava Universe — Golden Dragon Empire Administrative Atlas

**Status:** Owner-authorized worldbuilding addition based on the established world framework.  
**Scope:** Official working roster of regional jurisdictions, prefectures, county seats, and empire-wide administrative totals.  
**Population baseline:** `world/WORLD.md` estimates the Empire at **320 million**. Regional population shares below are planning estimates that sum to that established total; they are not a claim that a census has already been shown in-world.  
**Authority:** This atlas is the single source for administrative names, territorial-scale estimates, and the named village list. The governance framework lives in `world/GOLDEN_DRAGON_EMPIRE_GOVERNANCE.md`; regional modules hold local implementation, selected settlements, and NPC registries.

## 1. Empire-wide Administrative Totals and Territorial Scale

The Golden Dragon Empire is a **vast territorial state**, not a compact cluster of densely packed provinces. Its working territorial estimate is approximately **12.8 million km²** for a baseline population of 320 million. The area figures are broad worldbuilding estimates, not surveyed borders or a claim that every square kilometre is settled.

**Important settlement-count rule:** the 22 villages are the **named, notable rural villages currently catalogued for roleplay**, not a claim that only 22 rural communities physically exist across 12.8 million km². Unnamed hamlets, farmsteads, seasonal camps, and small settlements may exist but are not individually listed or assumed to have named institutions/NPCs. This replaces the old 50,000-village and 9,200-township/district bookkeeping totals, which were too granular for the current world map. The register now contains 33 prefectures, 268 counties, 361 cities, and 1,838 towns. Thirteen former prefectures were consolidated into neighboring prefectures within their own regions; their former seats remain local cities or administrative centers where listed, not independent prefectural seats. Cities and towns remain broad planning estimates, not exhaustive named-settlement lists.

These administrative and territorial figures are owner-authorized working estimates. A city may be administered directly by a prefecture or the central government; a town is normally under a county. Military posts and clan estates are not counted as civilian settlements. Household registers remain a population-record unit, not a separate territorial tier.

## 3. Centralization, Administrative Authority, and Naming Rules

1. **Development follows proximity to the imperial center.** Huanglongcheng and the Longyuan core have the most complete roads, civil offices, schools, medical care, markets, skilled services, and public infrastructure. Access and quality generally decline with distance from the capital and major regional seats.
2. **Outer regions are not uniformly prosperous.** Remote counties and villages may rely on family networks, local artisans, village elders, travelling healers, caravan traders, and community labor. Government services may be infrequent, understaffed, delayed, or absent. A wealthy port, mine, or furnace district can still exist inside a region with neglected hinterlands.
3. **Centralization is a gradient, not a hard border.** A regional seat or major trade route can be comparatively prosperous even far from Huanglongcheng, while isolated settlements near the center may remain poor. Do not assume every resident experiences the region's average wealth or service access.
4. **Huanglongcheng** is the imperial capital and seat of the central government in Longyuan. It is not an eighth region.
5. Each regional governor oversees the region's prefectures. Each surviving prefecture coordinates its assigned county jurisdictions, generally seven to ten counties after consolidation; each county coordinates its registered cities and towns. Local arrangements may vary when a major city is directly administered at a higher level.
6. County seats retain their established names even where their former prefecture was consolidated; those jurisdictions were reassigned to a surviving prefecture in the same region. County names may therefore reflect historic administrative origins rather than the current prefectural seat.
7. The atlas currently catalogues 22 named rural villages across seven regions. These are the main rural locations intended for ordinary roleplay, not a census of every hamlet or farmstead in the Empire. Do not generate hundreds or thousands of named villages by default; add another only when a scene or owner request needs it, and record it in the matching regional module.
8. Official positions are separate from clan influence and noble status. A clan's specialization does not prove that it governs the matching region or prefecture.
9. Military garrisons and fortresses are recorded separately from civilian administrative totals. Baiyue has a greater frontier-defense focus, but the number and names of individual garrisons must be recorded in its regional module when developed.
10. If a later event changes borders, a prefecture seat, or the count of a jurisdiction, update this atlas first, then synchronize the affected regional module and `world/MAP_AND_REGIONS.md`.

## 4. Naming Logic by Region

- **Longyuan:** courtly, ceremonial, and imperial-administration names.
- **Canglan:** rivers, waterways, fertile plains, granaries, and transport crossings.
- **Jinyue:** jade veins, ore, stone, and mineral resources.
- **Tianfeng:** scholarship, texts, lectures, academies, and elevated terrain.
- **Yanling:** furnaces, embers, volcanic rock, and metalworking.
- **Baiyue:** passes, walls, watchposts, ridges, and frontier logistics.
- **Xingluo:** stars, tides, harbors, sails, salt, and coastal navigation.

These naming patterns reinforce existing regional identities; they do not imply that every resident shares a single occupation or that the relevant clan controls the area.
