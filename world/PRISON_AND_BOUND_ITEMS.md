# Merzkava Universe — Prison Systems and Bound Items

**Status:** Canonical world-system reference.
**Scope:** Restraints used by prisons, courts, military custody, sects, and clandestine detention facilities.
**Mechanics boundary:** This module establishes item categories, intended functions, risks, and custody context. It does not invent universal numeric penalties, guaranteed effects, or an item's tier. Specific effects must be supported by the item's canonical entry, a verified appraisal, or a properly established in-scene discovery.

## 1. Core principle

A prison restraint is a **bound item**: an object attached to, worn by, or bound to a detainee to restrict movement, power, access, or behavior. It may be a traditional restraint, an Artificial Artifact manufactured by a Arcanist, an Ancient Artifact, or a Relic. These origins are not interchangeable. An item is not automatically an Artificial Artifact merely because it uses formations, nor is it automatically a Relic because its effects seem mysterious.

Restraints can be combined when their functions are compatible. They are not universally interchangeable: a device that suppresses Martial Soul activation does not necessarily suppress physical strength, and a boundary seal does not necessarily prevent speech, thought, or escape through every possible method.

## 2. Main restraint categories

### 2.1 Martial Soul Suppression Chain

A chain, shackle, or linked restraint designed to disrupt or suppress access to a detainee's Martial Soul.

- Possible purpose: restrict Martial Soul manifestation, activation, or use while the restraint remains active.
- Possible presentation: heavy links, engraved locking plates, seal-tags, or a fitted restraint connected to an anchor.
- Limits: does not automatically erase the Martial Soul, remove cultivation, seal every Spirit Ring, or suppress unrelated physical abilities. Exact reach, activation conditions, and resistance depend on the individual item.
- Risk: prolonged or excessive suppression may cause pain, fatigue, disorientation, or other documented adverse effects, but none is universal without an item-specific rule.

### 2.2 Sealing Collar / Ornamental Bound Collar

A collar shaped like jewelry or an accessory—such as a torque, pendant collar, choker, neck ring, or decorative clasp—used to conceal or normalize a restraint.

- Possible purpose: restrict Martial Soul access, trigger a warning, enforce a geas, monitor a detainee, or link the wearer to a ward.
- Its ornamental appearance does not establish its function. A plain-looking collar may be harmless, while an elaborate one may be a restraint.
- Possible consequences include pressure, fear, intrusive whispers, a forced return signal, or activation of another restraint. These effects are item-specific, not guaranteed.
- Removal may require a key, authorized release, a counter-formulation, or disabling the linked seal. Do not assume every collar is impossible to remove.

### 2.3 Physical Suppression Chain

A traditional or artifact-assisted chain intended to suppress bodily strength, speed, or exertion rather than directly sealing a Martial Soul.

- Possible purpose: prevent a physically powerful detainee from overpowering guards, breaking restraints, or forcing open doors.
- Extreme examples may press the wearer's physical output down to an almost helpless level—described colloquially as “no stronger than a baby”—but that is a severe effect of a particular device, not the baseline effect of every chain.
- It may affect movement, balance, breathing, stamina, or muscle control if the item description explicitly establishes those effects.
- It does not automatically lower Spirit Realm, remove knowledge, erase techniques, or suppress Martial Soul powers unless those functions are separately established.

### 2.4 Boundary-Binding Token

A bracelet, anklet, collar, seal tag, ring, or other bound object keyed to a prison ward or defined perimeter.

- Possible purpose: prevent a detainee from crossing a marked boundary, leaving a cell block, entering a forbidden wing, or moving beyond a custody zone.
- A boundary can be physical, formation-based, or linked to a specific ward. Its actual perimeter must be established; never invent invisible walls or map edges.
- Crossing or attempting to cross may trigger an alarm, return pulse, pain, paralysis, disorientation, fear, or another consequence only when that behavior is defined for the item.
- Boundary enforcement is not universal teleportation and does not prove that the perimeter is escape-proof.

### 2.5 Fear-Inducing Restraint

A restraint whose design or linked formation imposes dread, panic, or an aversive response when a condition is met.

- Possible purpose: deter escape, intimidation, compliance enforcement, or psychological control.
- Possible signs: dread near a threshold, shaking, a feeling of being watched, or fear triggered by disobedience.
- Fear is not proof of guilt, and resistance to fear is not proof of innocence.
- Do not automatically equate fear effects with mind control, memory alteration, or reliable truth detection.

### 2.6 Whisper-Seal / Delirium Restraint

A restraint associated with intrusive whispers, disordered perceptions, or mental pressure.

- Possible purpose: punish, destabilize, discourage escape, or make prolonged confinement more difficult.
- Possible effects include whispers, hallucination-like experiences, confusion, insomnia, or difficulty concentrating, if specifically supported by the device.
- Such effects may be unreliable, damaging, or deliberately abused. They do not provide trustworthy evidence, reveal truth, or establish that the detainee is actually insane.
- No restraint automatically grants access to a detainee's memories or thoughts.

### 2.7 Qi- or Spirit-Realm Suppression Seal

A seal designed to constrain Qi circulation, restrict access to cultivation techniques, or limit output within a stated range.

- It may be used against high-realm cultivators whose ordinary shackles would be insufficient.
- A realm-suppression seal is not automatically a realm reduction: the detainee's recorded Spirit Realm remains unchanged unless a canonical mechanic says otherwise.
- Suppression strength, compatible targets, duration, backlash, and failure conditions must be defined for the particular item.

### 2.8 Traditional restraints

Iron shackles, ropes, locked stocks, manacles, cages, weighted chains, and other non-artifact restraints remain viable, especially for ordinary detainees.

- Their effectiveness depends on material, workmanship, fit, guard practice, and the detainee's actual abilities.
- Traditional does not mean harmless, and artificial does not mean infallible.
- A restraint's appearance alone does not prove whether it is ordinary equipment or an artifact.

## 3. Artificial and traditional restraint distinction

| Aspect | Traditional restraint | Artificial Artifact restraint |
|---|---|---|
| Main basis | Material strength, locks, physical construction, established techniques | Manufactured functions, formulations, formations, or linked mechanisms |
| Common use | Ordinary prisoners, temporary custody, basic movement restriction | Specialized custody, Martial Soul or Qi suppression, boundary enforcement, monitoring |
| Failure modes | Broken links, lock failure, poor fit, guard error | Depleted or disrupted power, formulation failure, incompatible target, damaged components, countermeasures |
| Appraisal | Material and construction inspection may suffice for ordinary equipment | Function, tier, maker, power source, limits, and risks may require appraisal |
| Reliability | Depends on construction and custody conditions | Depends on design and conditions; never presumed absolute |

Ancient Artifacts and Relics may be used as restraints if a specific example is established, but their origin category alone does not prove any binding function.

## 3A. Abuse of restraints against half-beasts

Bound items are also a known instrument of exploitation. Corrupt elites, officials, wealthy patrons, and criminal operators may misuse Martial Soul suppression chains, ornamental collars, physical-suppression chains, boundary seals, or other restraints to capture and enslave half-beasts.

**The three major powers—Golden Dragon Empire, Tianhai Yunzhou, and Beiming Gu—prohibit the enslavement of half-beasts.** This legal prohibition does not mean abuse has disappeared: corrupt actors may conceal captives, falsify custody records, disguise restraints as jewelry, or exploit weak enforcement. Such abuse is especially common in the Lawless Territories, where no single authority consistently enforces the three powers' laws and coercive markets can thrive.

As a result, half-beasts are rarely seen openly in Lawless Territories unless they are powerful enough to defend themselves, evade capture, or make would-be captors think twice.

The Lawless underworld's organizations and networks are the largest suppliers of enslaved half-beasts to corrupt buyers tied to all three major powers. Their cross-border trade is a **public secret** in underworld and corrupt elite circles, sustained by brokers, intermediaries, falsified custody records, and selective enforcement. All three powers officially prohibit enslavement; this canon describes corrupt individuals and networks evading or violating that ban, not an official state policy. Do not assume every Lawless organization participates: specific groups' roles require established evidence.
 This is a broad regional pattern, not an absolute rule: hidden captives, escaped victims, protected residents, and weaker half-beasts may still exist. A half-beast's presence or absence does not by itself establish their history, strength, or legal status.

These facts do not make every restraint a slave device or every holder an enslaver. The item's function, the captor's conduct, and the individual's circumstances must be established separately.

## 4. Detainee population and custody status

Prisons can hold people with very different backgrounds, and the alleged offense does not determine the truth of the charge.

- **Cultivators and rogue Spirit Realm experts:** detained for alleged unlawful techniques, violence, forbidden experiments, factional conflict, or other offenses. Their actual realm and capabilities must be verified; “rogue” is a label or allegation, not proof of guilt.
- **Corrupt ministers and officials:** may be held for bribery, embezzlement, abuse of authority, or political crimes. They may also be protected, selectively prosecuted, or falsely accused by rivals.
- **Ordinary offenders:** pickpockets, thieves, smugglers, brawlers, fraudsters, and other civilians may be detained using proportionate traditional restraints or stronger devices when a documented risk justifies them.
- **Political prisoners and framed detainees:** may be innocent, wrongly identified, framed, silenced as witnesses, or held through manipulated evidence and corrupt process.
- **Dangerous but not convicted detainees:** a credible immediate risk may lead to temporary restraint, but custody is not itself a verdict.

A prison may be lawful, corrupt, faction-run, military, secret, or privately operated. Its authority and procedures must be established locally; there is no single universal prison authority across Merzkava.

## 5. Use, escalation, and consequences

1. Select a restraint according to the detainee's verified capabilities, the actual risk, and the institution's established rules.
2. State the restraint's function and relevant limits when known. If the item is unidentified, use the canonical unknown-item label and seek appraisal rather than inventing effects.
3. Escalating from ordinary shackles to Martial Soul suppression, physical suppression, boundary-binding, fear effects, or whisper-seals is a meaningful custody decision with potential consequences.
4. Severe physical or psychological effects are possible for specific devices, but they are not automatic features of all prisons.
5. Attempting to cross a boundary, remove a collar, break a chain, disrupt a formation, or resist suppression is an action with an outcome determined by the item's established mechanics, the detainee's capabilities, and the scene—not an automatic success or automatic failure.
6. A failed escape attempt does not prove guilt. Successful escape does not prove innocence.
7. A restraint cannot reveal guilt, establish truth, or validate a charge unless a separate, explicitly established system supports that claim—and even then its reliability and limits must be respected.

## 7. Canon boundaries

- A bound item may restrict movement, physical strength, Martial Soul access, Qi, cultivation techniques, behavior, or a defined perimeter—but no single item is assumed to do all of these.
- “Spirit Realm expert” describes cultivation status; it does not make every such detainee immune to restraint or automatically easy to suppress.
- Suppression does not silently change a detainee's Spirit Realm, Martial Soul identity, Spirit Rings, memories, or guilt status.
- Fear, whispers, delirium, pain, weakness, and boundary penalties are possible effects of specific devices, not universal prison mechanics.
- Prison population, named facilities, item inventories, officers, legal procedure, and local corruption are regional facts and must be sourced or established in-scene.
