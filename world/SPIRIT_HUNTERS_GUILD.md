# Merzkava Universe — Spirit Hunters Guild

**Status:** Official organization concept and operating rules established by this module.  
**Scope:** General Guild contracts and jobs, six difficulty tiers, temporary party registration, Spirit Beast expeditions, member misconduct, blacklisting, and Guild bounty/Deadpool notices.  
**Mechanical authority:** `core/SPIRIT_RING_RULES.md`, `bestiary/SPIRIT_BEAST_RULES.md`, `gameplay/COMBAT_SYSTEM.md`, `core/HP_SYSTEM.md`, and `core/INJURY_SYSTEM.md` remain authoritative for their respective mechanics.

---

## 1. Purpose and Reach

The **Spirit Hunters Guild** is a flexible contract and expedition organization, not a service limited to hunting Spirit Beasts. Depending on local demand and the office's reach, its board may list escort assignments, transport of important cargo, searches for specific items, reconnaissance, retrieval, protection, verified information gathering, Spirit Beast tracking/hunting, and other clearly described work. It gives people without academy, clan, sect, or powerful-household connections a way to find paid work and temporary companions. A contract's exact scope, reward, deadline, risks, and proof of completion must be stated or established in play.

The Guild is not a world government, a universal police force, or a guarantee of safety. Its offices, reach, reputation, staff, fees, membership rules, and authority vary by location unless established by a local canon entry or an actual event. Do not assume a branch exists in every city or village.

The Guild is distinct from any clan, sect, academy, state military, or Lawless Territory faction. Membership in one group does not automatically mean membership in another.

## 2. Temporary Registration and Mixed Parties

A person may seek **temporary registration for a specific contract or party** without becoming a permanent Guild member. This is especially useful for people with few reliable relationships or poor ties to their family, clan, sect, or academy. The Guild can assemble a temporary team from available people whose stated roles fit the objective. Temporary registration is not permanent membership and does not guarantee a place on every contract. Depending on local traffic and recruitment, a party may include:

- academy students on an approved outing or students seeking practical experience;
- younger members of clans or sects;
- independent cultivators, hired guides, and ordinary professional hunters;
- half-beasts and other intelligent peoples where they can join and communicate under the local conditions;
- people from Lawless Territories;
- low-ranking imperial personnel or members of the lower social circles of the Empire;
- strangers whose background or motives have not yet been verified.

This mixture is normal enough to be plausible, but the exact roster is decided by local availability, the expedition's requirements, and events. Do not force every listed background into every team, and do not treat any ancestry, faction, social class, or region as proof of trustworthiness or hostility.

Before a contract begins, the party should establish the objective, route, division of duties, expected duration, signals, emergency response, reward and cost split, cargo/item custody, evidence of completion, and who is responsible for decisions. For Spirit Beast hunts, they should also agree on Spirit Ring and other loot ownership. These are recommended safeguards, not a guarantee that everyone will follow them. A temporary registration verifies only details actually checked; it does not prove loyalty, strength, honesty, or harmless intent.

## 3. Six Guild Contract Difficulty Tiers

The Guild uses **six difficulty tiers** for contract listing and team matching. A tier describes the expected overall challenge and risk, not just the strength of a Spirit Beast. Escorting, cargo transport, item searches, reconnaissance, recovery, protection, and Spirit Beast work can all be listed at any appropriate tier.

| Guild tier | Typical scope | Usual minimum readiness guide |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 — Basic** | Simple local errands, low-risk escort, ordinary deliveries, searching for common items, reporting visible field signs, or assisting on a clearly bounded task. | **Martial Soul Awakening is required. A Spirit Ring is not required.** An awakened Mortal may apply if the role and situation fit. |
| **Tier 2 — Standard** | Longer or less predictable routes, routine cargo with some risk, escort through unsettled areas, searches requiring field skill, or a modest Spirit Beast assignment. | Usually a Spirit Realm 1 cultivator (Spirit Initiate) or a suitably capable awakened specialist for a non-combat role. |
| **Tier 3 — Challenging** | Risky travel, valuable cargo, hostile-area reconnaissance, difficult item recovery, protective work with a credible threat, or a hunt requiring coordinated combat. | Usually Spirit Realm 2 (Spirit Adept) or a team whose combined documented capabilities justify the contract. |
| **Tier 4 — High Risk** | Dangerous wilderness routes, high-value or sensitive transport, hostile retrieval, significant threats to an escort, or a demanding Spirit Beast hunt. | Usually Spirit Realm 3 (Spirit Elder) or a well-prepared party with relevant experience, equipment, and a credible plan. |
| **Tier 5 — Severe** | Major threats, remote or unstable routes, strategically important cargo, dangerous recovery missions, or hunts likely to overwhelm ordinary teams. | Usually Spirit Realm 4–5 (Spirit Monarch or Spirit King), preferably with complementary roles and relevant experience. |
| **Tier 6 — Extreme** | Exceptional threats, extremely dangerous expeditions, critical cargo or recovery under severe opposition, or hunts where a mistake may be catastrophic. | **Normally suited only to experts at Spirit Realm 6 or above (Spirit Ancestor+).** Lower-Spirit Realm participants are not automatically forbidden, but must not be treated as capable without a concrete, credible reason and a team/plan that addresses the gap. |

### Tier assignment and eligibility

- The tier is assigned from the actual objective, threat evidence, route, time pressure, cargo/item value and sensitivity, opposition, environmental hazards, and consequences of failure. Do not assign a tier solely from the reward amount or from the presence of a Spirit Beast.
- Spirit Realm guidance is a **readiness guide**, not an automatic success formula or an absolute legal restriction. Evaluate each participant's actual abilities, experience, role, equipment, injuries, knowledge, and teamwork. A support specialist may be suitable for a high-tier team without being expected to duel its main threat.
- Guild offices may set stricter eligibility or team-composition conditions for a particular contract when the stated risks justify them. Those conditions must be visible in the listing or established in play; do not invent them after a character accepts.
- Tier 1's entry rule is explicit: the person must have undergone Martial Soul Awakening, but **does not need to have absorbed a Spirit Ring**. Awakening does not automatically make the person combat-ready; a Tier 1 contract still has to fit their actual role and capabilities.
- The tiers do not grant combat bonuses, Spirit Rings, compatibility, immunity, guaranteed payment, or plot protection. A team can fail even when its members meet the usual Spirit Realm guide.
- Do not invent universal success percentages, automatic reward amounts, fixed Guild fees, or a guaranteed number of available jobs. Local boards, fees, reach, contract volume, and reward terms depend on the established office and circumstances.
- Temporary party composition is flexible and may be randomly drawn from people actually available locally: academy students, young clan or sect members, independent cultivators, half-beasts, people from Lawless Territories, lower-ranking imperial circles, and strangers. Do not force all backgrounds into every team or equate background with morality.

## 4. Misconduct, Blacklisting, and the Deadpool Board

The Guild may blacklist a member or temporary registrant when credible evidence supports serious misconduct during an expedition, such as deliberately abandoning teammates in a known lethal situation, sabotaging the team, stealing an agreed share, staging an ambush against party members, or attempting to murder or exploit them. An accusation alone is not conclusive proof.

Depending on the severity, available evidence, and the local Guild's authority, responses may include a warning, loss of expedition privileges, suspension, permanent blacklisting, compensation demands, or referral to local authorities. A Guild cannot automatically enforce its decisions everywhere, especially in territories beyond its practical reach.

For severe cases, the Guild may post a **Deadpool Notice**: a bounty contract for the named offender, with the terms clearly stating whether the target is to be captured alive or may be killed. This is a dangerous escalation, not an automatic response to every dispute.

- The notice must identify the alleged offense, supporting evidence or unresolved uncertainty, the issuing Guild office or sponsor, the bounty, and whether the contract requires live capture or permits lethal force.
- The Guild must not invent evidence or declare a character guilty solely because another member accuses them.
- Whether a bounty is recognized, enforceable, or lawful depends on the local jurisdiction and the issuing party's actual reach. In Lawless Territories, such notices may be informal and especially dangerous; their existence does not make them lawful or universally accepted.
- A bounty makes the target vulnerable to bounty hunters and opportunists; it does not mean every hunter knows the notice, accepts it, or can find the target.
- False reports, forged evidence, revenge claims, and corrupt bounty sponsors are possible complications. The Guild's records and reputation may be imperfect.

## 5. Contract Safety and Responsibility

The Guild may provide route reports, guides, party registration, and agreed coordination, but it does not promise a particular level of escort, protection, or rescue unless explicitly contracted. A registered party can still be outmatched, separated, betrayed, misled, lose cargo or an item, or encounter changing conditions.

Party members must not be treated as interchangeable combat units. Their abilities, motives, knowledge, equipment, injuries, and willingness to cooperate are individual. A mixed party can be a valuable advantage or a source of conflict.

For Spirit Beast work, the Guild does not guarantee that a Beast will appear, that a target's age or tier is correctly known, that the hunt succeeds, that a ring belongs to a particular member, or that absorption succeeds. For any contract, completion and reward payment depend on the agreed terms and credible evidence of completion. Ownership and distribution of a ring or recovered property must follow an explicit agreement, applicable local rules, or the actual outcome; never assume the last hitter owns every reward if a valid prior agreement says otherwise.

## 6. Mechanical Limits

- Use `bestiary/SPIRIT_BEAST_RULES.md` and the actual habitat/field evidence to establish likely species, danger, and target information. Do not invent precise encounter probabilities.
- Resolve combat using `gameplay/COMBAT_SYSTEM.md`, with PowerScale and Qi from `core/SPIRIT_RING_RULES.md`; use HP and Injury modules when relevant.
- Calculate the target ring's compatibility using the official formula in `core/SPIRIT_RING_RULES.md`. Do not confuse the ability to defeat a Beast with the ability to absorb its ring.
- A failed hunt, unexpected Beast, betrayal, injury, lost equipment, or death can occur when supported by the actual situation. No route grants plot armor.
- Guild membership, academy training, clan status, or imperial affiliation does not create an unrecorded combat bonus or change Spirit Ring compatibility.
- Keep rumors, accusations, bounties, and private motives separate from facts known by the player character. A character must have a plausible way to learn about a notice or a person's reputation.
